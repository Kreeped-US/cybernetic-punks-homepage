## 0. The corrected thesis (why this exists)

The reason this site deserves to exist is **verified synthesis: recommendations WITH RECEIPTS**,
built ON a reference substrate we maintain because that substrate is the moat's foundation.

Three corrections are baked into that sentence, each replacing a flawed claim from the first draft:

1. **"Reference is lost" is false as a law.** It is a per-game judgment. True at CoD scale, where
   entrenched high-DA sites own every lookup. FALSE for a niche launch: Marathon's own entity
   (reference) pages are the site's BEST-performing URLs, and Wardogs reference is thinly covered by
   anyone today. So we do not concede reference as a channel by default -- we judge it per game.

2. **Reference is not a competing CHANNEL; it is the SUBSTRATE synthesis runs on.** The solver
   computes FROM the verified store. A synthesis page's authority is the receipts chain that runs
   down to the entity rows behind every number it cites. Synthesis without a maintained reference
   layer is, in Fable's words, "a vibes list with math-shaped decoration." So we MAINTAIN the
   reference store -- not to publish price tables that out-reference wardogshub, but because it is
   the foundation the synthesis (and its trustworthiness) stands on.

3. **The differentiator is the receipts, not the page count.** The interactive widget does not rank
   (a solver behind a form is one near-empty URL to Google). Synthesis reaches people two ways --
   as shareable artifacts and as crawlable pages (Section 3) -- and both are worth building because
   each carries the receipts chain competitors decline to build, not because either produces a large
   number of pages.

This reframes the whole document: not "how many programmatic pages can we sprout for traffic," but
"how does verified synthesis reach players, on a young domain, without tripping the scaled-content
silhouette -- while we maintain the reference substrate that makes the synthesis trustworthy."

## 1. THE QUERY UNIVERSE (web-researched, grounded -- with a demand-audit caveat)

The task was explicit: do not assume the queries have volume; check. Every axis below is backed by
at least one competitor already ranking a page for it. The pattern is unambiguous: **the loadout/
build space is where the guide sites concentrate, and they already segment it by role AND budget AND
level** -- the same axes our solver multiplies.

**Axis D -- Budget x Role together** (the multiplied query, proving intersections are searched):
- Skycoach, "WARDOGS Best Loadouts: Weapons, Items for **Every Role and Budget**"
  (https://skycoach.gg/blog/wardogs/articles/wardogs-best-loadouts)

The pages titled "for Every Role and Budget" and "by Role & Budget" are the key finding: **the market
searches the intersections**, and competitors answer them with one hand-written omnibus article.

### 1.2 The demand-audit caveat (Fable's correction -- read this before trusting 1.1)

A competitor ranking a page proves the QUERY exists. It does NOT prove OUR page will rank for it.
Most of the sites in 1.1 are established, higher-DA guide domains (skycoach, dexerto, sportskeeda,
metaforge). A high-DA site publishing programmatic-shaped pages proves **programmatic pages CAN rank
in this niche** -- it does NOT prove a young, low-DA domain can publish the same shape and survive.
High-DA sites absorb a scaled-content-silhouette risk (Section 5) that we cannot absorb yet. So:

- The evidence in 1.1 validates DEMAND and validates the AXES. It does not validate our RANKING.
- Our ranking is gated on domain authority, which is why the community-distribution channel
  (Section 3.1) -- the DA lever -- is sequenced first.
- Treat every "competitor X ranks this" as "the query is real," never as "so we will rank too."

### 1.3 Grounded facts that make the axes REAL (not cosmetic)

The axes only produce distinct answers if the economy and progression genuinely change the answer
across cells. They do:

- **Progression gates the roster:** AK74 @ Assault 3, SKS @ Recon 5, RPG-7 @ Support 5, MP5 @ Medic
  15, M249 @ Support 15, M4 @ Assault 20, MK22 @ Recon 25 (per the unlock guides). The roster at
  level 5 is a strict subset of the roster at level 25. Career level (sum of six class levels) drives
  account-wide unlocks: armor tiers, backpacks, sidearms, ammo.
- **Economy gates the build:** L3 armor $2,000, L3 helmet $1,500; a poverty kit ~$180/life; AMR 50
  ~$8,800 / 12.5 kg. So "$500 sniper loadout" has no real build; "$3,000" and "$8,000 assault
  loadout" genuinely differ. Reported principle: "armour applies before ammunition in the damage
  calculation" -- so on a small budget the right answer is often *buy armor, not a better gun*, a
  non-obvious synthesis a table cannot make.
- **Weapon anchors circulate:** T-21 best all-round (~$600), Super-45 best SMG (~1200 RPM, .45 ACP),
  GGX-class sidearms (~$200), SVD as practical Recon, M4 (~800 RPM) better *after* Assault 20. These
  are creator/community/beta-attributed, NOT owner-verified -- which is why provenance matters
  (Section 6).

## 2. THE HONEST VALUE CASE (modest-and-compounding, not a bonanza)

The first draft called this an "SEO bonanza" and committed to "~150-250 pages." Both are the exact
overclaim pattern we veto in tier-list content: an unverified superlative and an uncommitted volume
number presented as a result. Corrected framing:

### 2.1 The long-tail barely exists yet
The game launched TODAY (2026-09-10). The long-tail search space for "best cheap assault loadout at
level 20" forms over WEEKS as players climb, hit budget/unlock walls, and search. Per-query volume
for the deep leaves is on the order of tens/month, forming gradually. Anyone projecting a page count
or a traffic number on launch day is guessing -- so this doc commits to NO volume number. **The
demand map (real GSC impressions, Section 5) decides the size. We do not.**

### 2.2 Why build it anyway: reuse economics + intent quality
The honest justification is NOT traffic size. It is two things:

1. **Reuse economics.** The engine already exists -- the Marathon advisor core (`generateBuild`),
   the SSR-canonical render, `build_pages` persistence, the freshness check, the confidence-tier
   components, and (net-new but one-time) the Wardogs solver + gates. Once built, the marginal cost
   of the next synthesis page is ~zero. A modest per-query return clears a ~zero marginal cost. The
   return does not need to be a bonanza; it needs to beat approximately nothing, and it does.
2. **Exceptional intent quality.** A searcher typing "best assault loadout on a $3k budget at level
   20" IS a Build Advisor user -- the query is the product spec. This is the highest-intent traffic
   the site can attract: not a drive-by lookup, but a player asking exactly the question the solver
   answers. Low volume x high intent x ~zero marginal cost is a sound build even if the volume is
   modest for months.

### 2.3 The structural advantage over Marathon's advisor (kept, reframed)
This part of the original analysis holds -- reframed as "more addressable intent," not "more pages."

Marathon's advisor reasons on ONE axis (effectiveness); `weapon_stats` has no cost/unlock column, so
nothing constrains it. Its synthesis space is `shell x weapon`, one kind of question. Wardogs adds
three constraint axes:

```
Marathon:  effectiveness                                          -> optimize             (1 axis)
Wardogs:   effectiveness x cost x progression x playstyle x class -> optimize UNDER CONSTRAINTS (4+)
```

Constraints are what create long-tail intent. Nobody wins "best gun" (head term); "best cheap
assault gun at level 20" is winnable BECAUSE it is specific -- and it only exists where a budget and
an unlock state exist. Marathon has neither, so that intent does not exist for it; Wardogs has both,
so it does. This is a genuine structural advantage in the SHAPE of addressable intent. It is not a
promise of volume, and it is realized only as the long-tail forms (2.1) and only as DA permits
ranking (1.2).

## 3. THE TWO DISTRIBUTION CHANNELS (synthesis reaches people two ways)

The first draft named only programmatic SEO. Fable's most valuable addition: synthesis has a SECOND
channel, and on a young domain it is the PRIMARY near-term play because it builds the DA the SEO
channel is gated on.

### 3.1 Channel A -- COMMUNITY DISTRIBUTION (primary near-term; the DA lever)
Synthesis artifacts -- a specific solved build, a loadout card, a role/budget ranking -- are the most
LINKABLE, SHAREABLE assets the site produces. A math-backed, receipts-carrying Wardogs build is
exactly the kind of thing that gets posted and argued about where the players are (Reddit, Discord,
YouTube comment sections, forum threads) on launch day. Each share that earns a link RAISES DOMAIN
AUTHORITY -- which gates ranking (1.2) and widens the thin-content risk tolerance (Section 5).

- **On launch day, a shareable build posted "where the arguments happen" earns more than 200 pages
  waiting for a crawl.** The crawlable grid cannot rank on a domain with no authority; a shareable
  artifact can travel on its own merit and BUILD that authority.
- The artifact is the same solver output as a crawlable page -- so this channel is not extra work,
  it is a second surface on the same engine (reuse economics again): a permalinked build card, made
  to be screenshotted and linked.
- Honest/receipts framing is the shareability edge: "here's the build AND the sourced numbers behind
  it, and here's what's still attributed-not-verified" is more credible -- more linkable -- than a
  confident unsourced list.

### 3.2 Channel B -- PROGRAMMATIC SEO GRID (DA-gated; evidence-ramped)
The crawlable synthesis pages (Sections 4-5). This channel is real and compounding, but it is GATED
on the DA that Channel A builds, and it must be grown by evidence, never sprouted (Section 5). It is
the long-term compounding surface; Channel A is the near-term ignition.

### 3.3 The sequencing
1. **Near-term:** ship the solver + a shareable build artifact surface (Channel A) + the canonical
   HUB pages (a small, substantive set -- not the grid). Distribute artifacts in-community to build
   links/DA.
2. **As DA and GSC-demonstrated demand accrue:** promote individual variant leaf pages (Channel B),
   one cohort at a time, on evidence (Section 5.1). The 150-200 figure from the first draft is
   re-cast as a CEILING that evidence may eventually justify -- never a launch size, never a target.

## 4. PROGRAMMATIC PAGE-GENERATION STRATEGY (Channel B mechanics)

### 4.1 URL structure (human-readable, query-shaped, hierarchical) -- unchanged, it was right
```
/wardogs/loadouts                                  Root hub ("wardogs best loadouts")
/wardogs/loadouts/[class]                          Class hub    ("best assault loadout")
/wardogs/loadouts/[class]/[budget]                 Class x budget ("best cheap assault loadout")
/wardogs/loadouts/[class]/level-[band]             Class x level  ("best assault loadout level 20")
/wardogs/loadouts/[class]/[budget]/level-[band]    LEAF: the deep long-tail intersection
/wardogs/loadouts/weapons/[slug]                   Crawlable per-weapon synthesis (reuse pattern)
/wardogs/tools/loadout                             The interactive widget (does NOT rank; feeds both channels)
```
- `[class]` = `assault | medic | recon | support | driver | pilot`
- `[budget]` = `budget | standard | kitted | max`
- `[band]` = `5 | 15 | 25 | 40 | 50` (unlock-inflection bands, labeled by ceiling)
- Playstyle is an on-page toggle, NOT a URL segment (avoids cosmetic-variant URLs; see Gate 5).

### 4.2 Rendering (reuse Marathon + Bodycam patterns)
- **SSR canonical pages** via `generateStaticParams` over the EVIDENCE-PROMOTED set (Section 5.1),
  NOT a launch-day full grid. Reuse the Marathon SSR-canonical render and the Bodycam crawlable
  pattern (`app/bodycam/weapons/[slug]/page.js`).
- **Persistence + freshness:** reuse `build_pages` + `maxUpdatedAt`; reuse
  `lib/advisor/regenerateCanonical.js`. Regenerate when the store or solver output changes.
- **The widget feeds both channels:** it produces the shareable artifact (Channel A) and deep-links
  to the canonical page (Channel B) for a produced intersection.

### 4.3 Page template (the substantive-content floor -- see also Section 5)
Every page carries, in order: (1) query-matched H1/title; (2) the solved loadout card (specific
weapons/prices/total, from `lib/wardogs/loadoutSolver.js`); (3) **the WHY** (solver rationale + LLM
prose -- the synthesis reference sites lack); (4) tradeoffs (what you gave up at this budget/level);
(5) unlock prerequisites (class-level gates); (6) budget breakdown; (7) alternatives / adjacent cells
(internal links, 4.4); (8) sources + tiered provenance badges + the "prices may change, verify
in-game" note; (9) JSON-LD (`Article` + `ItemList`).

### 4.4 Internal linking for topical authority
Leaf -> up (class/budget/level hubs); leaf -> sideways (adjacent budget tier, adjacent level band,
same-cell sibling classes); leaf -> weapon (each recommended gun's crawlable page); hub -> down (its
leaves); root hub -> all class hubs + widget. Every anchor is a real loadout query -- a coherent
topical cluster. NOTE: the mesh only exists among PROMOTED pages (Section 5.1); we do not pre-wire
links to unpromoted variants.

## 5. THE THIN-CONTENT GUARD: DOMAIN SILHOUETTE (make-or-break)

The first draft's guard was per-page and therefore INSUFFICIENT. Fable's core correction: Google's
post-March-2024 scaled-content posture evaluates the SITE'S SILHOUETTE, not just each page. A young,
low-DA domain that SPROUTS a burst of ~200 templated URLs matches the abuse shape **even if every
page passes per-page distinctness.** Per-page distinctness is NECESSARY, NOT SUFFICIENT. So the guard
has two layers: per-page gates (5.2) AND domain-level requirements (5.1). Both must hold.

### 5.1 Domain-level requirements (the layer the first draft missed)

**(a) Evidence-promoted ramp -- we GROW pages, we never SPROUT them.**
Ship canonical HUB pages first (a small substantive set). A variant/leaf page is generated and
indexed only when it has EVIDENCE for existing: GSC-demonstrated demand (real impressions for that
query pattern) AND solver output-distinctness (5.2). 150-200 is a CEILING that evidence may reach
OVER TIME, never the launch size. This inherits the Marathon build-generator's evidence-ramp
discipline: pages earn their existence; they are not minted on spec.

**(b) Corpus ratio -- programmatic pages stay a MINORITY of a substantive corpus.**
If synthesis pages become most of a game's URLs, the silhouette is the problem regardless of per-page
quality. The Wardogs vertical must carry a substantive non-programmatic corpus (editorial, reference
substrate, guides) that outweighs the programmatic grid. Monitor the ratio; if the grid approaches
majority, stop promoting and grow the substantive corpus first.

**(c) Intent-distinctness -- distinct from solver-distinctness.**
Two pages whose BUILDS differ but whose SEARCHERS type the SAME query are thin-BY-INTENT and
cannibalize each other. Pruning must be INTENT-based, not merely "the solver produced a different
build." Axes must map to DEMAND-DIFFERENTIATED queries. If "$3k assault L20" and "$3.5k assault L20"
are the same search intent, they are ONE page even if the solver would allocate the $500 differently.
Solver-distinctness (5.2, Gate 2) is necessary; intent-distinctness is the additional test.

**(d) Engagement -- pages must be USED, not just distinct.**
A distinct page nobody dwells on, links to, or reaches is still a silhouette liability. Promoted
pages must show engagement (dwell, internal-link participation, some external reference). Pages that
never earn engagement are candidates for de-indexing/collapse -- the grid is pruned by real use, not
only by generation-time logic.

### 5.2 Per-page gates (necessary, not sufficient -- run these on every candidate)

- **Gate 1 -- Demand:** the cell maps to a query pattern validated in Section 1 (and, per 5.1a,
  GSC-demonstrated before promotion). No invented axes.
- **Gate 2 -- Solver-distinctness:** the solver produces a MEASURABLY different build (different
  primary weapon, OR armor tier, OR materially different allocation) than already-promoted neighbors.
  Non-distinct cells collapse into the broader page and `rel=canonical` to it.
- **Gate 3 -- Honest-null = NOINDEX/non-render (HARD RULE, corrected).** Where the solver returns no
  valid build (e.g. "$500 sniper loadout," "max-tier at level 3"), the result is **NEVER a published
  "no build found" page** -- that is the thinnest possible content and the first draft was wrong to
  propose an indexed honest-null redirect. Solver-returns-no-valid-build -> the URL is NOINDEX or
  simply not rendered; the intent is served by the relevant HUB, not by a dedicated null page. Honest
  nulls live in the interactive widget's response and in shareable context, never as indexed URLs.
- **Gate 4 -- Substantive floor:** ships only with the full template (4.3): solved build, the WHY,
  tradeoffs, unlock prereqs, cited sources. A noun-swap template does not clear the floor.
- **Gate 5 -- Canonicalization:** playstyle variants and near-identical cells resolve to one
  canonical URL. Prevents cannibalization and thin-variant sprawl.

### 5.3 How we get durable value without the silhouette risk
Volume is never the goal (Section 2). Durable value comes from: shareable artifacts building DA
(Channel A) -> evidence (GSC demand + engagement) promoting individual pages one cohort at a time
(5.1a) -> each promoted page passing both intent-distinctness (5.1c) and the per-page gates (5.2) ->
the grid staying a minority of a substantive corpus (5.1b). The result is a grid that GREW on
evidence, is mostly-used, and never presented a sprout silhouette. We would rather have 40
evidence-earned pages Google trusts than 200 sprouted pages that flag the domain.

## 6. PROVENANCE: CREATOR-SOURCED, INHERITED (EEAT signal + moat)

Provenance is doctrine AND an SEO asset -- with a correction to how the substrate is labeled.

### 6.1 The substrate is creator-sourced, and the solver inherits it
Swoleguy's ballistics data is **CREATOR-SOURCED**: it is legitimate to use ONLY because his in-game
testing is the primary record that exists -- there is no first-party ballistics data yet. So:
- Store rows carry `verified_source` = the creator's artifact, **attributed per claim, NEVER
  game-verified.** First-party data SUPERSEDES the moment Bulkhead publishes.
- **The solver's outputs INHERIT the provenance of their inputs.** A build computed from attributed
  data is itself ATTRIBUTED, not verified. A synthesis page does not get to launder attributed
  inputs into a confident claim; its confidence tier is the floor of its inputs' tiers.
- Label the substrate honestly and the synthesis stays honest automatically. This is the receipts
  chain from Section 0: page claim -> solver -> store row -> creator artifact (or Bulkhead, once it
  exists).

### 6.2 Provenance as an EEAT / quality signal
Every page and every shareable artifact cites its inputs with the shared tiered marks
(`components/network/confidenceTiers.js`): combat data attributed to Swoleguy's testing,
economy/progression to Bulkhead's confirmed figures, each with its tier (verified / attributed /
partial / pending). Google's quality systems reward demonstrated expertise and trust; a page that
shows where each number came from and how confident it is beats a competitor's unattributed
assertion -- and is the shareability edge in Channel A (Section 3.1).

### 6.3 Honesty as differentiator, provenance as moat
The research surfaced competitors' own tell: prices are unpublished and "anyone doing that right now
is guessing." Competitors assert exact numbers as fact; we tier attributed-vs-verified, carry the
"verify in-game" note, and never publish a fabricated build (Gate 3). This honesty compounds
(users and Google learn the site is reliable) and is defensible: the reference model (scrape, assert,
copy) cannot adopt it without undercutting its confident-answer positioning. The moat is not the
build recommendation (copyable); it is the maintained reference substrate + solver + receipts chain
that the recommendation stands on (Section 0) -- the wedge competitors decline to build.
