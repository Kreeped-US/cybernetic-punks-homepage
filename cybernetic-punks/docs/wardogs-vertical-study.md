## 1. What Wardogs is (the facts)

**Identity:** A "tactical all-out warfare" FPS. 100 players split across **three teams** fighting over a randomized central "Control Zone." Developer **BULKHEAD**, publisher **Team17**. Built in Unreal Engine 5. Set in the derelict industrial mountains of Eastern Europe (fictional, modern-Georgia-inspired). Explicitly **NOT a battle royale and NOT an extraction shooter** — the studio has repeatedly said so. Closest analogy: a persistent-economy take on King of the Hill with combined arms.

**Release:** Steam **Early Access on September 10, 2026** (some sources say Sep 11 — the exact day should be verified against the live Steam page; this discrepancy itself is worth noting). PC/Windows only at launch. Console (Xbox/PS5/Switch 2) planned for ~2027, undated. Studio expects **1-2 years in Early Access** before 1.0. Price **$39.99** EA (studio has said the price will rise at full launch), plus a one-time **$49.99 Supporter Edition**.

**The core mechanic — the cash economy (this is the important part for us):**
- Every player starts each match with **$10,000** (community-wiki reported; verify).
- **No fixed classes.** You buy a loadout each life — weapons, gear, utility, vehicles — and what you bought *is* your role for that life. Die, and you re-buy, no obligation to repeat.
- The studio references a class *framework* as starting points: **Infantry, Medic, Pilot, Builder, Engineer** — but these are fluid, not locked. (superseded by wardogs-firstparty-VERIFIED.md)
- **Cash is earned by teamplay**, not just kills: reviving squadmates, transporting friendlies to the zone, holding the objective. "Cash is king."
- **Cash and profits persist match to match** (a persistent economy — losing a tank actually costs you).
- Loadout purchases feed **XP across 6 progression tracks** (community-reported; verify).
- **Control Zone** scoring: first team to 100 points wins; a "Hot Zone" inside the Control Zone counts double.
- **Building & destruction:** forward operating bases, mortar stations, anti-air, sniper towers; destructible battlefield (with deliberate caps on how much map you can level).
- Combined arms: infantry, vehicles, helicopters, logistics, proximity voice chat.
- A **"Black Market"** out-of-match cash-spending system is confirmed-but-not-yet-live.

**Monetization stance (this matters for brand alignment):** Bulkhead has hardened an aggressively anti-monetization position: "No Battlepass. No Pay to Win. No Nikki Minaj Skins. No Bullshit. Earn cosmetics by playing." Confirmed: no monetization during Early Access, in-game cash/gold never purchasable, camos never directly buyable. That's an unusually player-first, anti-hype posture — **culturally aligned with CNP's own "no hype, just intel" ethos.**

## 2. Why this is a strong fit (the case FOR)

**A. The traction is real and large — this is not a niche gamble.**
- Passed **1,000,000 Steam wishlists** before the August beta (studio-confirmed).
- The **Aug 21-23 closed beta drew nearly 500,000 players**, peaking at a reported **~105,774 concurrent** on SteamDB on Aug 23 — reportedly beating Squad and Arma in concurrents.
- It ran the *same weekend* as the Call of Duty: MW4 beta and the two **briefly sat as the top two sellers on Steam overall** — a genuinely surprising result for a new IP from a relatively unknown studio going toe-to-toe with Activision.
- This is a game with serious pre-launch momentum and a large, engaged, hungry-for-info audience — exactly the audience CNP converts.

**B. The timing is a rare window — and it aligns with the DMZ launch.**
- Wardogs EA launches **Sep 10, 2026**; DMZ launches **Oct 23, 2026**. These are ~6 weeks apart. CNP is already gearing up its whole machine for a fall extraction-shooter launch (DMZ). Wardogs lands *first* and warms the exact same audience.
- Getting in at **Early Access launch** — before the data settles, before competitors establish themselves — is the single best moment to plant a verified-data flag. First-mover on verified intel for a game that doesn't have its numbers pinned down yet.

**C. The mechanics are a data goldmine — and the moat mechanic is native.**
- **The cash economy IS a verification problem.** The entire game revolves around cash costs (weapon prices, vehicle prices, gear costs, teamplay payouts) and — critically — **Bulkhead has NOT published a single weapon/vehicle/gear price.** Community sources are explicitly saying "treat any dollar figure you see as unofficial until confirmed in-game." That is *precisely* the CNP wedge: a game whose core system is numbers nobody has verified yet. A verified Wardogs economy database (real loadout costs, real payouts, real break-even math) would be the definitive resource, and it maps directly onto your existing DMZ economy-editor concept (BROKER).
- Rich entity surface for verified tables: **weapons, vehicles, helicopters, gear/utility, buildables (FOB/mortar/AA/sniper tower), the 5 role archetypes, the 6 XP tracks, map/Control-Zone data, the cash economy.** This is at least as data-rich as Marathon, arguably richer (the economy layer is deeper).
- "Can you convert your time into money faster than they can?" is the game's own framing — an *optimization* game, which is what intel sites exist to serve.

**D. Competitive landscape is wide open — and full of exactly what CNP beats.**
- The current Wardogs "data" sites are a swarm of low-quality SEO wikis (wardogshub.gg, wardogsdb.com, war-dogs.wiki, wardogswiki.org, wardogs-wiki.online, wardogshandbook.com, neonsect.com, etc.) — **the exact AI-generated-aggregator slop your root page positions against.** Several are already hedging that they can't confirm numbers. None has a first-party verified-data moat. A human-verified Wardogs economy/stats resource would stand alone in a field of guessers.
- No dominant, authoritative, verified source has emerged yet. The niche is unclaimed.

**E. Brand/cultural alignment is unusually strong.**
- Bulkhead's "no battlepass, no P2W, no bullshit, earn it by playing" stance is *the same anti-hype, player-first, substance-over-monetization ethos* as CNP. Covering Wardogs is on-brand in a way that isn't just opportunistic — the game's values match the network's.

## 3. The honest risks and counterarguments (the case AGAINST / cautions)

**A. It's NOT an extraction shooter.** CNP's two current verticals (Marathon, DMZ) are both extraction shooters — that's a coherent niche. Wardogs is a 100-player combined-arms objective shooter. Rolling it in **broadens the network from "extraction-shooter intel" to "competitive-shooter intel"** (which your root positioning — "competitive shooters" — already supports). But it's a real scope decision: is CNP the *extraction* intel network, or the *competitive-shooter* intel network? The root already says the latter, so Wardogs fits the *stated* positioning — but it's worth being deliberate about.

**B. Early Access = shifting sands.** The game launches in EA and expects 1-2 years there. The economy is explicitly "the system most likely to get rebalanced first once real players are spending." So verified data will have a **shorter half-life** than a stable game — numbers will change often, requiring more frequent re-verification. This is *manageable* (your pipeline is built for patch tracking) but it's a higher-maintenance vertical than a frozen game like Marathon. **The upside:** constant change is constant content, and constant re-verification is exactly where your verified-moat beats static aggregators.

**C. Resource load during the most critical window.** CNP is a solo operation, pre-launch on DMZ (Oct 23), mid-way through a root-page rebuild, with an ingestion rebuild still finishing. Adding a third vertical **6 weeks before your primary growth priority launches** is real load. Wardogs EA (Sep 10) lands *before* DMZ (Oct 23) — so the question is whether spinning up Wardogs *steals focus* from DMZ or *warms the runway* for it. Honest answer: it could do either depending on how it's scoped.

**D. Data-access reality.** You can't verify Wardogs data until you can play it (EA, Sep 10). Pre-launch, everything is Activision-style "scaffold-safe systems" (confirmed mechanics) vs. "beta-observed/datamined numbers" (unverified). Same provenance discipline as DMZ: build the infrastructure and the confirmed-systems content pre-launch; the verified numbers come after you're in the game. The August beta produced community numbers, but those are unverified and will change for EA.

**E. Console delay.** PC-only until ~2027. Your audience is PC-first anyway, so this is minor, but it caps the near-term addressable audience somewhat.

## 4. Verdict

**Roll it in — but scope it deliberately, and do NOT let it derail DMZ.** The case is strong: real traction (1M wishlists, ~500k beta players), a wide-open verified-data niche full of the exact slop CNP beats, a core cash-economy mechanic that is *native* to your verification moat and your existing economy-editor concept, strong brand/cultural alignment, and a rare first-mover window at EA launch. The strategic logic is nearly identical to the DMZ bet, and Wardogs actually has *more proven pre-launch demand* than most launches get.

The risk is not "is Wardogs worth covering" — it clearly is. The risk is **sequencing and load** against the DMZ launch. The answer is to treat Wardogs as a **low-cost infrastructure-first scaffold now**, riding the infrastructure that already travels per-game, and *not* a full editorial spin-up that competes with DMZ for attention.
